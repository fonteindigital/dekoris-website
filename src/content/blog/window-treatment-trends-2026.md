Trends in interiors move more slowly than fashion, and that is not a bad thing. What emerges in a given year usually reflects a genuine shift in how people want to live rather than a seasonal novelty. These five directions are worth paying attention to in 2026 — each one is practical, considered, and well-suited to the way Namibian homes are actually used.

## 1. Natural textures

Woven and textured fabrics have been growing steadily, and they are firmly established now. Linen, cotton-linen blends, and materials with a visible weave read as honest and unforced. They work particularly well in Namibian interiors because they complement the earth tones that are already part of our landscape — the ochres, tans, and warm greys that appear in our stone, our soil, and our architecture. A Roman blind in a natural linen fabric does not compete with its surroundings. It belongs.

## 2. Motorised solutions

Motorisation was once associated with large commercial projects or very high-end homes. That has changed. The technology is now accessible at a residential level, and it makes practical sense in a climate where large glazed openings are common. Being able to lower blinds across an east-facing window before the morning sun builds, without leaving your bed, is a genuine quality-of-life improvement. We expect to see more Windhoek homes incorporating motorised blinds over the next few years.

## 3. Earthy palettes

Cool greys and stark whites have given way to warmer tones. Terracotta, warm sand, deep olive, and muted clay are appearing in window treatments across the board. For Namibian homeowners, this is an easy transition — these are colours that already feel at home here. A Zebra blind in a warm stone tone, or a curtain in dusty rose, connects the interior to the light and landscape outside in a way that a neutral grey never quite manages.

## 4. Layered treatments

Single-layer window dressing — one blind or one curtain — is being replaced by a more composed approach. Layering a sheer or screen roller blind with a heavier curtain panel gives you flexibility across the day and a more finished look overall. It also makes practical sense: the sheer handles daytime glare, the curtain provides full privacy and insulation at night. The result feels considered rather than decorated.

## 5. No-drill installation

This is less a visual trend than a structural one, and it matters particularly for renters. No-drill systems use tension or adhesive mounting to secure a blind without touching the wall or frame. As more people in Windhoek rent their homes, the ability to dress windows properly without landlord permission — or the risk of losing a deposit — has real appeal. The finished result is clean and looks the same as a drilled installation. At Dekoris, it is one of the options we hear about most often.

These directions are not prescriptive. They are simply where considered homeowners are moving, and most of them have a practical logic behind them that makes them worth understanding.
